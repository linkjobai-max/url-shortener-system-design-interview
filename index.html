Designing a URL Shortener: A System Design Interview Walkthrough

The URL shortener is often the first system design question candidates see. It's small enough to finish in 45 minutes but rich enough to show how you think about scale.

1. Clarify requirements

Ask about read/write ratio (usually heavily read), expected volume, custom aliases, expiration, and analytics. Write the answers down; they drive every later choice.

2. Estimate

At 100 million new links a month and a 100:1 read ratio, you're near 40 writes and 4,000 reads per second. Seven Base62 characters give about 3.5 trillion codes, which is plenty.

3. Generate codes

Hashing the long URL risks collisions. A cleaner option is a distributed counter or pre-allocated ID ranges per server, encoded in Base62. Mention both and pick one with a reason.

4. Store and serve

A key-value store maps code to URL. Put a cache in front, since a small fraction of links gets most of the traffic. Return a 301 or 302 redirect depending on whether you need click analytics.

5. Talk about failure

What happens if the cache dies, or a region goes down? Interviewers reward candidates who bring this up without being asked.

Practicing this flow out loud a few times makes a huge difference. A tool for live interview coaching can play the interviewer and push back on your estimates the way a real one would.
